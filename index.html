Why another simulator?

Most drone datasets use motion-capture systems to record "ground truth" data. While these systems are great for slow movements, they struggle to accurately track drones during fast, aggressive maneuvers. They end up smoothing out the data, which hides important details.

Because of this, if your algorithm fails, you can't tell if the bug is in your code or if the dataset's ground truth was just inaccurate to begin with!

This simulator completely solves that problem. By mathematically generating the flight paths instead of measuring them in the real world, the ground truth is always 100% accurate, no matter how fast the drone is flying. This lets you debug your algorithms with absolute confidence.

What it gives you

Everything a VIO frontend needs, on the same clock, with the answer key attached.

◎

Analytic depth

Scenes are piecewise-planar, so every pixel's depth comes from a closed-form ray–plane intersection. No z-buffer quantisation, no rasterisation error.

↻

Angular rate you control

Peak rate is an independent variable you sweep, not a property of a recording you happened to capture. Reproduce the same manoeuvre at 1 or 12 rad/s.

◐

Real motion blur

Blur is produced by integrating true sub-exposures, so it varies across the image the way projective flow actually does — not a fixed convolution kernel.

⬡

Fisheye optics

An equidistant model with a ~115° field, matching the lenses racing drones actually fly. Pinhole assumptions break here — which is the point.

◈

Inertial data

Gyroscope and accelerometer with white noise and bias random walk, calibrated against measured in-flight noise rather than the datasheet.

⌁

Event stream

Log-intensity threshold model with refractory period and per-pixel threshold jitter, generated from high-rate renders.
