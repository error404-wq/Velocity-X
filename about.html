What I work on

Visual-inertial odometry works well until a vehicle starts moving aggressively. At a few hundred degrees per second, camera frames blur past the point of usefulness, feature correspondences survive only a handful of frames, and the assumptions that hold a state estimator together quietly stop being true.

What interests me is the part that is hard to see: when a system fails under those conditions, it is often unclear whether the algorithm is wrong or the measurement of it is. That question is what this simulator exists to answer.

How this project happened

It did not start as a simulator. It started as an attempt to build a better sensor-fusion policy for drone racing footage — and it kept failing in ways that turned out to be more interesting than the original goal.

Trying to make fusion work

Several weighting strategies for blending camera and gyroscope data. Each was reasonable on paper; each failed to improve accuracy. Four independent failures with the same shape is usually a sign that the problem is not where you are looking.

Building an instrument instead

If you cannot trust the measurement, build one you can. That meant a simulator where depth and pose are analytic — exact by construction rather than recorded — so that errors have exactly one place to come from.

Finding what was actually wrong

The simulator immediately exposed two problems in the earlier work: a lens model applied incorrectly, and a ground-truth reference too heavily filtered to resolve the motion being measured. Both had been quietly corrupting every result.

Opening it up

The instrument turned out to be more broadly useful than the result it was built to check, so it is open source and runs in a browser.

How I like to work

Test the risky assumption first, and cheaply. Most ideas that sound good are wrong, and the useful skill is finding that out in an afternoon rather than a quarter. Several ideas behind this project were killed within hours of being proposed — that is on the research page too, because negative results with a diagnosed mechanism are worth publishing.

The corollary is being honest about what a result does and does not show. Every number on this site is reproducible from the repository, and the validation page is as specific about the simulator's limits as about its strengths.
